# Part B — AI Research Assistance Reflection

### B1. How I Used AI During Research
* **Tools Used:** I primarily used ChatGPT-4o and Perplexity AI for the research phase of this solo project.
* **Tasks Performed:** I used Perplexity to track down dates and legal summaries for recent mid-2026 cases, specifically the final court approval of the $1.5 billion *Bartz v. Anthropic* settlement. I then used ChatGPT to help brainstorm how to condense complex legal concepts (like "transformative fair use") into simple bullet points for my 3-minute video script.

### B2. What Worked Well
* **Most Useful Output:** The most helpful task the AI performed was simplifying dense legal jargon into plain English suitable for a general audience.
* **Specific Example:** I pasted a massive, multi-page court summary regarding the June 2025 *Kadrey v. Meta* fair use decision into ChatGPT and asked, "Explain why the judge ruled this was 'highly transformative' in two sentences for a college freshman." It instantly gave me a clear, concise explanation that I adapted for my final video recording.

### B3. What Did Not Work Well
* **Errors or Limitations:** While AI is great for summarizing, it frequently hallucinated or mixed up specific dates, financial figures, and procedural statuses of ongoing litigation.
* **Specific Example:** When I asked ChatGPT about the *Bartz v. Anthropic* case, it initially told me the settlement was finalized and paid out in late 2025. I had to double-check this against actual legal news sources and corrected the script when I discovered the final approval order was actually just signed by the judge in July 2026. Relying solely on the AI's initial output would have made my presentation factually inaccurate.

### B4. Overall Reflection
* **Impact on Workflow:** Using AI as a research assistant significantly sped up my workflow. It allowed me to tackle a dense legal topic independently without getting bogged down in reading hundreds of pages of raw court documents.
* **Future Use:** If I did a similar project next time, I would still use AI for script outlining and jargon translation, but I would completely avoid using it as a search engine for hard facts. Next time, I will pull the primary sources first and only feed those specific texts into the AI to be summarized.
* **Biggest Takeaway:** The most important thing this experience taught me is that AI is a powerful processor of information, but a highly unreliable source of truth. You still need strict human oversight to verify the facts, especially with rapidly changing current events.
